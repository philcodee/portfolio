The Problem

Why This Existed

Cover Whale — a B2B Insurtech MGA serving Insurance Agents buying Commercial Trucking Insurance across most of the US — was switching its backend from a legacy PHP app to a Policy Admin System. Both the Agent (external) and Admin sides (CX, Underwriting, Ops, Product) were experiencing a consistent stream of data inaccuracies and vital information getting lost in the mix. The migration was the opportunity to fix it.

The Goal

Improve Policy Workflows for Agents and Admins. Bring a Dashboard experience to Agents for the first time — giving them a visually quantified view of their activity. For Internal Stakeholders, especially Underwriters working the platform all day, this meant Process Automation and Quality Control by surfacing Ineligible Risk earlier. For Agents, it meant cycling through more potential clients. Both translated directly to Revenue, Retention, and stronger Carrier Relationships.

The Constraint That Shaped Everything

No PAS Vendor had been selected yet. The full scope of available APIs, integrations, and how core processes like Endorsements would be carried out was unknown. The framework had to be built on best available understanding while remaining flexible enough to pivot. This required a massive cross-functional effort to maintain a single Source of Truth document — and keeping it current was an ongoing challenge throughout.

The Approach

Strategy

Make decisively broad strokes to reach Internal and External Stakeholder feedback as rapidly as possible. Lower fidelity kept the focus on ideas rather than execution, and kept Engineering, Underwriting, and Leadership in a position to flag feasibility issues before they became costly.

Lessons & Trade-offs

Trade-Off Made

The originally proposed architecture included both an Agent View and an Agency View. Cutting the Agency View meant rethinking the framework mid-project. Despite being a difficult call, it was the right one — a leaner scope accelerated implementation without sacrificing the core value to users.

What I'd Do Differently

Narrow the view of what a default-level dashboard should look like through additional research earlier in the project. Data frameworks need to exist before a meaningful Front End feature like a Dashboard can come to life — establishing those requirements sooner would have reduced the scope ambiguity that led to the Agent View / Agency View cut.

Takeaways

Flexibility at the onset of a large-scale project is non-negotiable. Not every Stakeholder need will be met in the first pass — and that's the point. Lower fidelity prototypes keep conversations focused on the right things, faster.
