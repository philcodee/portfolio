## Jordan Voss Read

This is the trickiest piece in the graduate section. Jordan lands on it with no context: "art school manifesto" could mean self-indulgent with no transferable signal. The job of the page is to flip that assumption in the first 10 seconds.

**What this piece actually shows a hiring manager:**
- Range — you work from concept to object to public installation
- Iteration instinct — 4+ formats tried before arriving at the right one
- Self-editing — started with 9 statements, shipped 2
- Awareness of audience — the park table failed, you named it, you moved on

**The risk:** If the page leads with the Dada reference or the manifesto text, Jordan bounces. It needs to feel like a designer who made art, not an artist who tagged "design thinking" onto it.

**The anchor:** The final distillation. Nine panels → two. That's the editorial muscle a hiring manager understands.

These are not traditional UX metrics — they're editorial choices that signal judgment. Jordan will read all three.

### 3 · The Brief + Approach (Panel)
- Left: brief in 2 sentences ("Write a manifesto. Choose typography that expresses it holistically.")
- Right: the Cheese Monkeys quote as pull-quote, and a single line on the Dada/Bread & Puppet reference
- No deep dive into the references — just enough to show the research instinct

### 4 · Iterations (Visual Grid — 4 cells)
Label each format concisely. Placeholder boxes for now (real photos can drop in later):
```
Book (w/ burn mark) | Tile Print (9 panels) | Paperweight + Letters | Park Table
```
One sentence per cell max. The arc reads visually — no captions needed beyond the label.

### 5 · The Manifesto Distilled (Inverted / Feature section)
The two surviving panels, large and clean — no context copy before them.
Let them land on their own.

> **Panel A:**
> Human experiences happen both with and without technology.
> I often interact with ghosts of a digital analog; paper becomes light.

> **Panel B:**
> I want interactions that are not for profit.
> **A public park, a hidden path, a simple surprise.**

Small label above: `From 9 panels, these two felt universal enough to share.`

### 6 · Outcomes (What I Got Out of It)
2-3 sentences max. Lead with the scaling insight — not the process. AI-assisted image generation callout belongs here as a specific, forward-looking note. End with the AI market image and the Dadaist hallucination caption — it earns a laugh and signals fluency.
